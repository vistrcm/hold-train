> Revised 2026-08-07 after plan review. Changes: palette split and both consumer renames merged
> into one green-ending task; `src/modules/written-test.ts` added as a second `renderHSI` consumer
> with an explicit no-nav contract; `OVERLAY_ALPHA` stays flat; several guards strengthened against

## Overview

The entry drill's compass instrument (`src/ui/hsi.ts`) has two defects, both reported by the user:

1. **It reads as an RMI, not an HSI.** The "course pointer" is a full-length magenta line drawn
   through the rose centre with no deviation bar, no TO/FROM flag and no nav-status field. That is
   an RMI needle's information content — "the course is oriented that way" — wearing an HSI's
   shape. It also does not look like the panel a student actually flies behind, which for this
   audience is a Garmin G1000 PFD.
2. **Every scenario implies the aircraft is directly over the fix.** The drill carries no position
   at all: `generateScenario` produces `seed → clearance + heading` and nothing else. A real
   holding clearance arrives *before* the clearance limit, with the fix some distance ahead.
   Training that always depicts the overhead moment is not representative of the moment the
   decision is actually made.

This plan rebuilds the HSI as a dark G1000-style PFD panel and gives the scenario a real position,
so the deviation bar and the distance field say something true instead of decorative.

**Non-goals** (explicitly out of scope, do not drift into these):
- The plan-view map (`src/ui/mapview.ts`) stays a light chart-like plate. It gains no aircraft
  position and no inbound track. Its only change here is a mechanical palette rename.
- No turn-rate indicator / trend vector (inert in a static scenario).
- No bearing pointer (BRG1) or its info window.
- No ARC/360 mode switch, no VOR/LOC green nav source — the nav source is GPS, always, magenta.
- The written-test entry figure gains **no** distance data (see Task 4's no-nav contract). Those
  are fixed content items; giving them a position is a separate decision.
- `determineEntry` is not touched. The answer key does not change. Distance and deviation are
  **display state only**; the AIM 5-3-8 j3 sector is decided by heading alone.

### Two accepted consequences, recorded so they are not "fixed" later

- **The deviation bar is pegged in the large majority of scenarios.** With any realistic lead
  distance, the needle is on scale only when the heading is within a few degrees of the holding
  course or its reciprocal. This is not an artifact of the chosen scale — an angular (VOR-style)
  scale pegs at 10° for the same reason. It is the physics of receiving a hold clearance while not
  established on the holding course, and it is the picture worth teaching: *the needle is pinned
  because you are not on the inbound course — that is what an entry is for.* Do not shorten the
  distances to make the needle livelier; that would trade a true picture for a busy one.
- **TO/FROM plus the bar's side together fix the quadrant of `heading − course`,** and two of the
  four quadrants determine the entry outright. No information is leaked — heading and course are
  both already on the rose — but it is a shortcut a student could adopt in place of the sector
  reasoning. Accepted: the shortcut is only available because the instrument is honest, and
  cockpit mode still withholds the drawn sectors and thumb band.

## Solution Overview

### 1. The nav picture (fixes defect 2)

The aircraft is **tracking direct to the fix**. That single assumption determines the whole
geometry from one new scalar, so nothing in the picture can contradict anything else:

```
fix at the origin; C = selected course, H = heading, D = distance to the fix

  aircraft lies at bearing  H + 180  from the fix, D NM out
  cross-track   xtk = -D · sin(H - C)      (+ = aircraft RIGHT of the course line)
  sense               TO  iff  |angleDiff(H, C)| < 90        (exactly 90 → FROM)
  needle offset       = -clamp(xtk, ±FULL_SCALE) · PX_PER_NM  (needle opposes displacement)
```

Worked sanity check, to be reproduced by hand in the Task 1 tests: `C = 000`, `H = 090` — the
aircraft is flying east at a fix that is therefore east of it, so it sits **west** of the fix. West
is left of a north-bound course, so `xtk = -D`, and following course 000 from there never closes on
the fix, so the sense is FROM at exactly the 90° tie-break.

`C` is the **inbound holding course** — the pilot has set it to visualise the hold — so the
lubber-vs-pointer relationship that *is* the entry technique stays on the instrument.

**Fidelity note (do not skip):** on a real G1000 the CRS knob is inoperative when GPS is the nav
source with an active leg, and a direct-to auto-slews the desired track so the CDI stays centred.
A manually selected course with a large deflection exists only in **OBS/SUSP** mode. The panel
therefore annunciates `OBS` — one text node — so the depicted picture is one that can actually
occur. This is consistent with content the app already ships: README line 12 lists "G1000 hold
behavior (SUSP, OBS holds)" in the oral bank.

Distance is generated from a **verified** ATC lead-time rule times a nominal groundspeed
(Task 7 verifies the rule against faa.gov before anything is rendered). No NM range is stated in
this plan on purpose — stating one here and asserting it in Task 7 is how the plan would end up
contradicting its own source.

### 2. The panel (fixes defect 1)

`renderHSI` draws a self-contained dark rounded panel that fills its whole viewBox, sitting on the
light page. Everything instrument-coloured is therefore measured against **one** backdrop, which is
simpler than today, where the lubber triangle and readout box straddle the face and the page.

```
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐   panel: pfd.face, rounded
 │ ┌HDG 041°┐            ▼      ┌CRS 300°┐ │   cyan bug value / magenta course value
 │ └────────┘         (lubber)  └────────┘ │
 │            · 33   N   3 ·                │   white card, ticks every 5°
 │        30 ·               · 6            │   labels every 30°
 │       ·        ▲(course arrow)     ·     │
 │     W ·        │                   · E   │
 │       ·    ● ● ┃  ✈  ● ●           ·     │   deviation bar slides; 4 dots
 │        24 ·    │ (TO/FROM ▲)  · 12       │
 │            · 21   S   15 ·                │
 │     GPS OBS   ZORVU   12.4 NM   TO       │   nav status line
 └──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Palette splits into two frozen groups — `PALETTE.plate.*` (the map, unchanged values) and
`PALETTE.pfd.*` (new, dark). The entry colours exist in both, and a hue-agreement test keeps them
the *same hue* so "green means direct" cannot come to mean two different colours on one screen —
which is the exact drift `PALETTE` was centralised to prevent, since the HSI and the map appear
together in the feedback view.

Digits render in a monospace stack so the readouts do not jitter as values change — an avionics
detail that costs nothing and needs no webfont.

## Technical Details

### New engine module: `src/engine/nav.ts`

```ts
export interface CourseDeviation {
  /** NM right of the selected course line (negative = left). */
  crossTrackNm: number
  /** TO when following the selected course from here closes on the fix. */
  sense: 'to' | 'from'
}
export function courseDeviation(heading: number, course: number, distanceNm: number): CourseDeviation
```

- **The docblock must state the direct-to assumption**, because the signature does not carry it:
  `sense` is derived from `heading` only, and that is valid *only* because bearing-to-fix equals
  heading under direct-to tracking. A future caller with a vectored aircraft would get a wrong flag.
- Validates both angles with `assertCourse` (rejecting `360` and negatives, per engine convention),
  not merely non-finites; rejects negative distance with `RangeError`.
- `distanceNm === 0` is decided and documented in Task 1: over the fix, `xtk` is 0 and `sense`
  reduces to a heading comparison with no geometric meaning. Prefer rejecting it.
- Pure; no clamping and no pixels — those are display concerns and live in `hsi.ts`.

### Display constants (in `hsi.ts`, exported for the tests to pin against)

| constant | source | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `FULL_SCALE_NM` | **verified in Task 7 or labelled unverified** | defines what a dot means; a student will read it quantitatively, so it is a user-visible aviation fact under the CLAUDE.md citation convention |
| `DEVIATION_DOTS` | 2 per side | dots at half and full scale |
| `PX_PER_NM` | derived from dot spacing | one source for bar offset and dot positions |

### Palette shape after the split

```ts
PALETTE = {
  plate: { face, bezel, ink, inkDim, readout, entry: { direct, teardrop, parallel } },  // values unchanged
  pfd:   { face, card, cardDim, chrome, bug, course, entry: { direct, teardrop, parallel } },
}

// FLAT on purpose. mapview.ts uses no alphas, so a `plate` group would be empty, and nesting
// would make `Object.keys(OVERLAY_ALPHA)` return group names — blinding the exhaustiveness guard
// that exists to stop an unmeasured alpha shipping. These are all PFD alphas; say so in a comment.
OVERLAY_ALPHA = { sectorFill, thumbBand }
```

`palette.test.ts` changes required by this shape (each is a hole if skipped):
- `leaves()` becomes **recursive** — it currently walks two levels and would silently skip every
  `pfd.entry.*` colour, i.e. the new ones.
- `TIERS` keys become 3-deep dotted; the "classifies every colour it declares" test then forces
  every new colour to be classified.
- Backdrop selection becomes **per group**, and that mapping needs its own exhaustiveness test —
  a hand-written group→backdrop table has exactly the failure mode `TIERS` has: it measures only
  the groups that existed when it was typed.
  - `plate.*` against `plate.face` and `--bg` (unchanged, still passes)
  - `pfd.*` against `pfd.face` only, plus a new assertion that `pfd.face` itself clears 3:1
    against `--bg` (SC 1.4.11 — the dark panel is its own component boundary, so it needs no bezel)
- The composited-overlay tests run against `pfd.face`. Note the asymmetry: a tint over a dark face
  *lightens* it, so the binding assertion is that white card marks stay ≥4.5:1 over a tinted wedge.

### Scenario change

`EntryScenario` gains `distanceNm: number`. Generated from a **new draw appended after** the
existing heading draw on the same decorrelated stream, so every existing seed keeps its heading —
which matters because stored misses and the Anki export are keyed by seed.

`store.recordMiss` and `missesToCards` are **deliberately not changed**: the entry answer is
rebuilt from `heading` + `holdSpec`, and distance is display state that does not affect it. Recorded
here rather than left unmentioned, because CLAUDE.md marks that snapshot/rebuild boundary as a
repeated failure site.

⚠️ **An alpha is tuned to a face, not to a palette.** Inverting the backdrop inverts which way a
tint moves it, so the value that was a *ceiling* on white paper became invisible on near-black: 0.1
renders the wedge at 1.15:1 against `pfd.face` — a wedge that is not there — while every existing
assertion passed comfortably, because all of them were ceilings. Hence a **floor**,
`tints the wedge enough to be seen at all` (1.3:1). It is deliberately **not** an SC threshold and
must not become one: a fill reaching SC 1.4.11's 3:1 against the face on its own needs α ≈ 0.44 and
puts the dim ticks crossing it at 2.2:1 (computed, all three colours). The fill and the boundary
cannot both carry the sector, which is *why* the boundary is stroked opaque — that split is
arithmetic, not style. The ceiling is `cardDim` at 3.79:1, reaching 3:1 at about α = 0.32.

`keeps ticks and labels legible over a tinted sector` became `keeps every card mark legible over a
tinted sector`, driven by a new `OVER_WEDGE` map with the same exhaustiveness guard `TIERS` and
`BACKDROPS` carry: raising `sectorFill` lowers the contrast of *everything* with a wedge under it,
not just the two marks that were hand-listed when the list was typed. `OVER_WEDGE` asks a different
question from `TIERS` and cannot reuse it — `pfd.course` is text in the CRS box, which sits on bare
face, but only a line on the card, so measuring it at its `TIERS` tier would demand 4.5:1 of a
needle (it has 3.92:1) and force the tint back down for a picture that never renders. `pfd.chrome`
is `null` for a reason worth keeping: it would fail at 1.96:1 if a wedge ever got under it, and the
only thing keeping it clear is that the card ring and box frames are drawn outside `LAYOUT.tickR`.

The other hole was at the DOM: `palette.test.ts` composites `PALETTE.pfd.entry.*` at
`OVERLAY_ALPHA.*` and judges the result, but reads `hsi.ts` only as source text — its scans are
satisfied by a wedge painted in `pfd.card` at `OVERLAY_ALPHA.thumbBand`, and then the measured
picture and the drawn one are different pictures. `overlay paint` in `hsi.test.ts` closes that.
