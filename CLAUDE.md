# hold-train — working rules

Holding-pattern trainer for the FAA instrument rating. Correctness beats features: an app that teaches a wrong entry sector is negative training.

## Engine is the oracle
- `src/engine/` (pure TS, no DOM) is the single source of truth for headings, sectors, clearances, wind. All modules consume it.
- Entry answers are NEVER hand-keyed — quiz/drill answer keys are generated by `determineEntry`. Dual-keyed answers ("parallel or teardrop") are allowed only when the engine reports a near-boundary heading (±5° per AIM 5-3-8 j8(d)).
- Engine changes require golden tests (hand-derived by drawing first — sign conventions invert easily) plus property tests (sectors sum to 360°; nonstandard mirrors standard; heading = inbound course → direct).
- `courseDeviation` (`src/engine/nav.ts`) is the oracle for everything the deviation needle shows — cross-track NM and the TO/FROM sense. `hsi.ts` adds only what the engine deliberately leaves out: `FULL_SCALE_NM`, dot spacing, pixels, and the clamp that pegs the bar on the outer dot. It assumes the aircraft is **tracking direct to the fix**, and that assumption is load-bearing rather than decorative: `sense` is derived from `heading` alone, valid only because bearing-to-fix equals heading under direct-to, so a future caller with a vectored aircraft gets a wrong flag. Sign convention, written out here because it inverts easily — the aircraft lies at bearing `heading + 180` from the fix; `xtk = -D·sin(heading − course)` with **+ = aircraft right of the course**; the drawn bar offset is the *negative* of that, because the bar shows where the course is, not where you are. `D = 0` is a `RangeError` alongside negatives and non-finites: over the fix the cross-track is 0 for every heading and the sense reduces to an angle comparison with no geometric meaning, so no caller may depict it. The course line has **two** ends and the aircraft is on it at both — `heading === course` (short of the fix) and its reciprocal (past it) — so the exact-zero guard covers both, and it is a guard rather than arithmetic because `Math.sin` gets neither end right: `+0` at 0° makes the product `-0`, and `Math.sin(PI)` is `1.2246e-16`, whose *negative* residual read as a real displacement to the left. Handling only the first shipped an HSI announcing "deviation 0.0 dots right" over a bar it drew dead centre, on ~1 seed in 360 (`docs/content-audit.md`, 2026-08-08). Its golden hid there because it was the one written `toBeCloseTo(0, 10)` while its twin at the other end was `toBe(0)` — pin an *on-the-line* claim exactly, since "nearly zero" is the defect. No epsilon, though: off those two headings a small cross-track is real. Downstream, `deviationText` picks its wording from the number it will **print**, not the raw value, so "centred" / "N dots" / "full scale" partition the pictures the panel can draw — round-to-nearest let 1.976 dots print `"2.0 dots"`, the clamp's own count, for a needle short of the peg. Never re-derive an expected deflection from `courseDeviation` inside an expectation — renderer and expectation flip together, which is the `turnToInbound` tautology one layer up; hand-derive the pixel offset from the drawn picture instead.

## Settled: the parallel entry's rejoin turn opposes the pattern
- The parallel entry's reintercept turn rotates **opposite** the pattern's turn direction (standard/right hold → **left** rejoin; nonstandard/left hold → **right**), crossing back over the holding course **toward the holding side**. Its first two turns — the fix-crossing turn onto outbound and the rejoin — both oppose the pattern; only the final turn on reaching the fix follows it. `PARALLEL_INTERCEPT_DEG = 45` in `src/engine/entry.ts` is the single source of the rollout convention (`C ± 45`, sweep 225°); `mapview.ts` and `sim.ts` import it. Before that both consumers hand-wrote the angle independently (a literal in `mapview.ts`, a private `PARALLEL_INTERCEPT_DEG` in `sim.ts`) and the engine had none; they happened to agree at 30. The 2026-08-02 branch raised the value to 45 for legibility and single-sourced it — that was a **change**, not the repair of a drift, so do not cite it as evidence 45 is the correct angle.
- **Do not re-derive this from AIM 5-3-8 j3(a).** Its "turn in the direction of the holding pattern through more than 180 degrees" is **spatial** (toward the racetrack), not rotational, and the sentence alone cannot settle it — this repo reasoned itself into the wrong answer once (`docs/content-audit.md`, "the parallel entry's reintercept turn was inverted app-wide", 2026-07-22) and shipped a track displaced ~2 turn diameters onto the nonholding side. **The ">180°" clause discriminates nothing**: computed correctly (updating the fix bearing for motion *during* the turn) both senses exceed 180° and are exactly equal at zero offset. Any argument resting on it is invalid by construction.
- The evidence is `docs/parallel-entry-evidence.md` — **two** FAA figures rendered and visually inspected (AIM FIG 5-3-4, AIP FIG ENR 1.5-3), plus ICAO Doc 8168 Vol I Pt I §6 Ch 1 ¶1.4.4/¶1.4.5 (mirrored verbatim in Vol II Pt II §4 Ch 1 ¶1.2.1.2/¶1.2.1.3), which says in words what the AIM says only in a picture — and says it *rotationally*, so it needs no interpretation at all: in a **right**-hand pattern the parallel rejoin is "turned **left** onto the holding side", while the teardrop is "turned **right**". ¶1.4.4(a)/(c) pin the other two turns as well (left across the fix, right only on second arrival), which is where the "first two turns oppose, final turn follows" structure above comes from. Cite it at that granularity: the pre-2026-08-02 "¶2.2.4/2.2.5, turned *toward* the holding side" was wrong on part, chapter, paragraph, edition year and wording, and pointed at a chapter about RNAV holding construction that has no such paragraph. FIG 5-3-4 is the decisive one: it draws the teardrop rejoin clockwise and the parallel rejoin counter-clockwise in the *same* standard-pattern figure. IFH Fig 10-6 is reported to agree but was **never rendered here** — cite it as second-hand or not at all; the dossier says so explicitly and four other artifacts contradicted it once. **Read that file instead of re-arguing from the AIM sentence.**
- Only the *direction* is evidence-backed. `PARALLEL_INTERCEPT_DEG = 45` is a drawing convention chosen for legibility — the AIM requires only ">180°", and the one rollout tangent measured in the dossier is ≈31°. Never justify the 45 by saying it matches the figures.
- Guarded by `src/parallel-entry.invariants.test.ts`: map sweep flag + rollout side vs the engine, the sim's flown rejoin *rotation* (integrated signed cross product of the track) vs the engine, and a `?raw` prose scan of every content module **and README** (globbed, not hand-listed). All three modules that once hand-wrote this geometry are covered — guarding two of three leaves the third free to go stale with the suite green. That same arithmetic caught the file twice more on 2026-08-02: the ICAO-citation guard harvested from a glob excluding `*.test.ts` while its docblock claimed three carriers, leaving the longest of them (`entry.test.ts`'s golden docblock) unscanned; and the prose scan globbed `src/content/*.ts` while README carried the fullest statement of the rule. A directory glob is exhaustive only over the directory it globs, and a scope narrower than the claim reads as coverage. That glob has now been widened twice for the same reason and is every non-test module under `src/` plus README: content-only missed README, and content+README missed `src/modules/`, where `flyItText` renders the rule into the drill feedback panel — the prose a *student* actually reads was the unscanned half, and a wrong gloss added there passed 602/602. Widening it required fixing the scan's unit, not just its scope: a line is a sentence in a single-line string literal and a README paragraph, but a **fragment** in an 80-column-wrapped comment, and a fragment can lose the very words that make it correct (`entry.ts`'s "the parallel rejoin counter-clockwise (against it)" breaks between "parallel" and "opposite"). Contiguous comment lines are therefore joined into one unit — which also strengthens the blacklist, since `WRONG` then scans the whole block. Do **not** tighten `CORRECT` to per-sentence: prose that *argues* the rule spreads it over sentences that are individually true and individually match nothing, so each failure is answered by widening the whitelist until it accepts anything. `WRONG` is the detector; `CORRECT` only asks whether the unit states the rule at all. `isRejoinAssertion` skips sentences naming no direction *value* (`PARALLEL_INTERCEPT_DEG`'s docblock is about the intercept **angle**), and that exemption is safe only because no wrong gloss can omit a direction — asserted over every known wrong wording, not assumed. Prose is normalised through `unemphasize` before matching, because both regexes match *contiguous* phrases and markdown splits them — `*against* the pattern` is invisible to the positive, and `*follows the pattern*` to the blacklist, which makes the guard strictly weaker on the one carrier a reader meets first. The negative regexes anchor on the *gloss* wording, never on the AIM quotation the strings legitimately carry verbatim — keep it that way, or the guard becomes satisfiable only by deleting the citation.
- Any test that derives its expected rollout heading from `turnToInbound` is a tautology (the outer sign cancels the inner one and both senses give 225°). Derive it from the *pattern* instead — see the ⚠️ on the A3 test in `src/engine/entry.test.ts`. The same trap catches the *consumers*, one level up: `mapview.ts` and `sim.ts` both read `turnToInbound` from the oracle, so an expectation that re-reads that field agrees by construction when the **engine** flips and pins only consumer-vs-oracle drift. Each consumer therefore needs one assertion with the settled rule *written out* against `hold.turnDirection` — the two `A2` tests in `src/parallel-entry.invariants.test.ts`, which are the only things in that file an inverted engine fails (mutation-confirmed 2026-08-03; the sim's was missing until then, and both docblocks claimed the opposite — that `sim.test.ts`'s goldens "pin the sim against itself", when their `RIGHT_NORTH`/`LEFT_NORTH` anchoring is precisely what makes them fail). A comment that misattributes which assertion catches which failure mode is worse than no comment: it argues the next session into deleting the one that works.
- `flyCircuit` (`src/engine/sim.ts`) starts the aircraft established **on** the inbound course at the fix, so its turn onto outbound is a full 180° and the parallel rejoin comes out *tangent* to the holding course rather than crossing it — the real maneuver crosses because the aircraft arrives at an angle from sector (a). Consequence: the `-2R` cross-track bound holds only *through rejoin completion*, and asserting it whole-track produces a red test that reads like a direction defect and argues the next session back into inverting the rejoin. The parallel branch is unreachable in the shipping app (`wind-lab.ts` only ever passes `entry: 'direct'`), and that bound is now asserted, not just stated, by `no shipping module calls flyCircuit with an entry other than direct`.
- Guarding a constant needs an assertion whose value *moves with* it. The rotation guard checked `Math.sign` and `> 180°`, which a 210° sweep satisfies, so `sim.ts` passed with `PARALLEL_INTERCEPT_DEG` swapped for a literal `30` — single-sourced in code, unpinned in assertion, whole suite green. Sign checks catch inversions and nothing else; pin the flown/drawn *heading or point* against the oracle. The same hole then turned up in the *map* half: sweep-flag + rollout-side are both sign checks, so only one hand-computed golden pair in `mapview.test.ts` held the drawn angle. It is now swept by `the drawn arc sweeps 180° + the engine intercept angle`, which reads the angle out of the SVG as `chord / radius = 2·sin(θ/2)` — scale-free, course-free, and no second copy of `mapview`'s geometry. Its tolerance is 2 decimals because the path rounds to 0.1 px on a ~13.6 px radius; an assertion tighter than the drawing's own resolution fails on rounding, not on defects. But a chord *length* is unsigned, and `sin(θ/2) = sin(180° − θ/2)`, so it fixes θ only **up to reflection about 180°**: 135° and 225° have the identical chord. Mis-signing the rollout to `c − PARALLEL_INTERCEPT_DEG·rs` sweeps 135°, keeps the sweep flag, still rolls out on the holding side — and left the whole invariants file green at 18/18, one `mapview.test.ts` golden pair again holding all twenty course × pattern cases. The companion `the drawn arc turns the oracle's way` closes it by measuring the chord's *bearing*: a chord subtends its arc symmetrically, so it runs at `outbound + rs·θ/2`, which is signed and still needs only the two endpoints already parsed. Reach for a *bearing or point* over a *length* whenever the defect can be a reflection. Mutation-test a new guard before trusting it.
- A source scan that checks the *file* is not a bound on the *call*. `no shipping module calls flyCircuit with an entry other than direct` first harvested every `entry: '…'` literal anywhere in a caller module: rewriting the call site to a non-literal left wind-lab genuinely flying a parallel circuit with the guard green (the file's other literals satisfied it), and an unrelated `entry: 'parallel'` object would have failed it spuriously. It now paren-matches each `flyCircuit(` argument list and rejects any call whose entry is neither an inline literal nor `textbookDecisions()` — whose own entry is pinned at *runtime*, since it lives inside the module the scan excludes. A guard that a refactor to a variable walks straight through only looks like coverage. That escape then reopened one level in, in the fix itself: reading only the *first* `entry:` match and short-circuiting on `textbookDecisions(` meant `{ ...textbookDecisions(h, w), entry: <non-literal> }` passed 18/18 while wind-lab genuinely flew a parallel circuit. A spread is a **default** and the key written after it wins, so the builder cannot excuse an entry — *every* `entry:` in the args is now required to be a quoted `'direct'`, and the builder only answers the "no `entry:` key at all" case. The paren scan is also lexer-unaware by choice (a `)` inside a string mis-slices it); it therefore throws on an unbalanced scan rather than returning the truncated slice, because a fail-open scan reports as a clean call.
- Test sample indices derived from constants must round **outward**. `REJOIN_END_SAMPLE` was exact only while `PARALLEL_INTERCEPT_DEG` stayed a multiple of the turn rate; at the ≈31° the dossier floats, `track[i]!` is `undefined` and the `!` converts a failed assertion into an opaque property-access crash while the rotation window silently truncates.
- An assertion that cannot fail is not documentation, it is fake coverage — delete the `expect` and keep the comment. Two lines in `entry.test.ts` (`normalizeDeg(140 − 45·pattern) === 185`, and the "inverted engine would sweep 135°" line, which is `360 − 225` by construction once the line above it passes) each carried a comment saying so and were still counted as tests.
- The parallel entry's *fix-crossing* turn is a function of the aircraft heading, not of `turnToInbound`. The two agree throughout the parallel sector, but not inside the ±5° teardrop/parallel band where parallel is still graded correct — `flyItText` in `src/modules/entry-drill.ts` therefore takes the heading and only claims "both turns opposite the pattern" when it is true of the scenario shown.

## FAA-source citation convention
- Every user-visible aviation fact carries a `source` field citing the FAA primary source at sub-paragraph granularity (e.g., `"AIM 5-3-8 j4"`, `"FAA JO 7110.65 4-6-4"`, `"14 CFR 91.185(c)(3)"`), rendered in the UI.
- Verify against FAA publications directly (fetch from faa.gov / ecfr.gov) — `docs/research-*.md` are an index into sources, not the authority. Unverifiable claims are labeled "unverified", never presented as fact.
- When a familiar teaching rule elaborates on AIM text but is not in it, render the AIM sentence verbatim inside the cited span and put the elaboration in a **separate** string explicitly labelled a derivation — `OUTBOUND_TIMING_AIM` vs `OUTBOUND_TIMING_NOTE` in `src/modules/entry-drill.ts`, pinned by `entry-drill.test.ts`. Never fold a derivation inside the quoted/cited span; presenting a derivation as citation is the failure the 2026-08-02 rejoin audit corrects.
- An **instrument constant can be a user-visible aviation fact**, and its citation then renders one module away. `FULL_SCALE_NM` in `src/ui/hsi.ts` is what a dot on the deviation bar is worth and a student reads the needle quantitatively — but `hsi.ts` draws no prose, so `FULL_SCALE_SOURCE` is rendered by the *consuming screen* (`CDI_SCALE_AIM`/`CDI_SCALE_NOTE` in `src/modules/entry-drill.ts`, quotation and derivation split as above, pinned by `entry-drill.test.ts`). A `*_SOURCE` exported with no rendering consumer satisfies nothing: `FULL_SCALE_SOURCE` shipped exactly that way, unrendered and unasserted, with the suite green (`docs/content-audit.md`, 2026-08-07). The coupling is **runtime-only** — no source grep sees it, the same class as the drill ids and `PHASE_PREP`/README below — so a new instrument constant needs its cite asserted on the screen that draws it. Assert the *rendered paragraph*, not the constants it was built from: `.entry-cdi` is composed from five strings and a scan of two of them lets a withdrawn claim back in through a sixth.
- An avionics **depiction** is not a content item and carries no `source` field: the `GPS`/`OBS` annunciators in `hsi.ts` are a fidelity decision (with GPS as the nav source and an active leg the CRS knob is inoperative and the CDI auto-slews, so a manually selected course with a live deflection occurs in OBS/SUSP mode and nowhere else). Its justification is Garmin, non-FAA, and lives in the `navStatus` docblock and `docs/content-audit.md` — not on screen, because the citation convention governs the *aviation facts* a screen asserts, and the drill's footnote is already at its budget. The same mode taught as a *fact* (the `g1000-obs-hold` oral item) does carry its Garmin source and software-version caveat; that is the line.
- Content items with known caveats (triple-vs-double drift, G1000 software-version features, ±5° tolerance) must surface the caveat in the UI. `OralItem` has a dedicated `caveat` field the oral-prep screen renders; the written bank and lessons carry theirs inline in the explanation/prose (e.g. `CHART_CAVEAT` in `src/content/questions.ts`). Never drop a caveat because the type has no field for it.
- Settled: **both sides of the holding course are protected airspace** — the non-holding side is the *narrower* primary area (38.5–40% of total width across all 28 current patterns), never "unprotected". Verified against FAA Order 8260.3G Table 16-6-1 in `docs/holding-protected-airspace.md` (which also derives the side mapping — the order never says "holding side" in the table). Orders 7130.3A and 8260.3B are cancelled; cite 8260.3G for holding criteria. `wind-lab.test.ts` pins the debrief wording and asserts "unprotected" is absent screen-wide; each content bank (`anki-deck`, `oral`, `questions`, `lessons`) carries its own test scan banning "unprotected" **and** the "protected side" framing that implies it without the word — a grep for the literal word alone missed the Anki card that taught the same model.
- Audit ledger: `docs/content-audit.md` — record any content fix there with its regression test. A prior ledger entry that turns out partly wrong is **amended in place with the counter-argument and citation**, plus a new dated correction entry — never silently edited past (the 2026-08-03 protected-airspace correction is the model).

## TDD policy
- Tests first, always: reference-case tests for engine and content modules, then implement until green. `npm test` (vitest) **and** `npm run typecheck` (`tsc --noEmit`) must both pass before a task is done — no exceptions. Vitest strips types with esbuild and never type-checks, so a green test run does not mean the code compiles; `tsconfig.json` runs `strict` + `noUncheckedIndexedAccess` + `noUnused*`, which is easy to trip in test files the suite happily runs.
- Content modules get validation tests (every item has source/ACS tag/answer; coverage per ACS element). UI modules get jsdom tests for logic (grading, state transitions), not pixel testing. No e2e framework.
- jsdom models a simplified cascade, so the style invariants a browser can silently break are asserted against the stylesheet **text** in `src/styles.test.ts` (`import CSS from './styles.css?raw'`): `[hidden]` must keep `display: none !important` (any author `display` rule outranks the UA rule — this once shipped the clearance-copy form visible while the clearance was still on screen), every `var(--x)` must be defined (a silent fallback is how the dashboard got a sub-4.5:1 "Ready ✓"); and the entry drill's answer group must clear the instrument above it by `margin-top >= 3 × gap` (`separates the answer group from the instrument above it`). That import is blank unless `vite.config.ts` keeps `test.css.include = [/styles\.css/]`, so the `is actually loaded` test is the guard against a vacuous pass.
- The answer-group ratio exists because the HSI panel rect fills its whole viewBox: with no separating margin each button sits 0 px from a filled, hard-edged region and 8 px from its neighbours, so the row reads as attached hardware — a bezel, a softkey strip — rather than as the answer input. The regression arrived through an SVG geometry constant in `hsi.ts`, one module away, with no CSS diff to show it. The margin rides on `.entry-answers-label`, **not** on `.entry-answers`: on the row it would open the gap between the label and the buttons it names, splitting the group instead of detaching it. **The 3x multiplier is a chosen convention with no published basis** — treat it exactly like `PARALLEL_INTERCEPT_DEG` and never attach a citation to it. Gestalt primary sources, Carbon, Atlassian, MIL-STD-1472G, HF-STD-001B and WCAG 2.2 supply no inter-group : intra-group ratio; Palmer's common-region work is demonstration-only and says outright that when grouping factors oppose there is "no way to predict the outcome", and the case here — an element flush against but *outside* a filled region — is unmeasured in all of it. The guard reads both lengths out of the stylesheet rather than retyping either, so it moves with the constants where a `toBe(1.5)` would not: keep both in `rem`, and keep `gap > 0` asserted, since a zero gap satisfies any margin at all and is the real vacuity hole. `remValue` refuses a foreign **unit**, not a `NaN` — a *lenient* parse is the green-test hazard (`margin-top: 24px` against `gap: 0.5rem` compares 24 to 1.5 and clears `3 × gap` sixteenfold while the drawn gap is smaller than the buttons' own), whereas returning `NaN` would already red the suite, since `toBeGreaterThan`/`toBeGreaterThanOrEqual` both fail on it. Do not restate the throw as protection against a green test; it buys a message about *notation* instead of one about spacing. The invariant has **two** sides and guarding the top one alone leaves the same defect reachable from below: `margin-bottom` must stay *under* the row's gap, or the label detaches from the buttons it names and becomes a third floating element with the 3x ratio still passing. And the ratio only closes the defect while the thing above the label is the instrument, which is a **DOM** fact no stylesheet assertion can see — `entry-drill.test.ts` pins `label.previousElementSibling` as `.entry-hsi`, so slipping a hint line in between (or moving the panel) fails there rather than shipping the buttons flush against the panel again. A margin also separates nothing on an element that generates no **box**, or one taken out of **flow**, and that half is invisible to the DOM test: `label.hidden` reflects the HTML attribute only, while every screen-reader-only idiom is CSS, so the stylesheet guard refuses a label rule declaring `display` or `position` **at all**. That is a *property* refusal and not a value list, because the value list is what shipped: `display: none|position: absolute|fixed` was the enumeration, and `display: contents` — which keeps the text and drops the box, so the margin vanishes with nothing on screen to show for it — walked straight through. Which `display` values still generate an in-flow box is a question the guard would have to model, and the label needs neither property, so a harmless `display: block` now reds too; that is the trade. Don't reword the DOM assertion's message back to "not a screen-reader-only name" — it cannot see one. `remValue` also takes the **last** declaration and refuses to read past a later shorthand (`margin`, `margin-block`, `column-gap`) rather than expanding it — a CSS parser's job, and one more thing to get subtly wrong. `all` belongs to **every** family and is appended rather than listed per-property: it is not a margin shorthand, which is exactly how the three hand-written families missed it, and `.entry-answers-label { all: unset }` shipped green against the real stylesheet with the label's margin gone. `ruleBlock`'s mechanisms each need their own case, and those cases must be **ordered so a leak would win**: the first synthetic put every decoy *above* the target rule, so the last-declaration read masked all of them and all four expectations passed with the leading boundary deleted outright — a guard whose own comment named mechanisms nothing exercised. Its model is flat rules + source order + last declaration, and each way out is loud: the prelude is split at its **top-level** commas (a selector list is this sheet's idiom in 14 rules, and a scan anchored on `<selector> {` sees the target only when it is written *last*, so which side of a comma someone typed on decided whether an override was visible); the selector is bounded by a trailing `(?![\w-])` and **no leading boundary**, because a class's own `.` delimits it and a leading one would hide the compound `.pill.entry-answers`; a rule whose *subject* merely contains the selector **throws** instead of being skipped, since `.entry-drill .entry-answers-label` outranks the flat rule whatever the source order and "it only applies inside `.foo`" is no reason to ignore a `.foo` that is always present; and every matching rule is collected, not the first. Only the subject counts, so `.entry-answers button` styles the button and is correctly ignored. But every one of those mechanisms reads selector **text** while the cascade matches **elements**, so a rule that reaches the element *without naming its class* was neither collected nor refused — invisible, and fail-*open*. `.entry-drill p` (this sheet's own idiom for `<p>` margins, four rules of it already), `#entry-answers-label` (that id exists only because `aria-labelledby` needs one) and `.entry-drill > *` each tie or outrank the flat class rule and therefore render; all of them, plus `.entry-drill div { gap: 0 }` on the row, were reproduced green against the real stylesheet with the ratio still reporting `1.5rem` against `0.5rem`. Deciding it needs facts CSS text does not carry — the element's tag, its id, its ancestors — so `ruleBlock` takes them as a `context` and `entry-drill.test.ts` pins them against the DOM (`carries only the names the stylesheet guard is told about`); a context that drifts from the markup re-opens the hole silently, which is why that pin exists rather than a comment. The comparison is against a single class's (0,1,0) — any id, class, attribute or pseudo-class in the member ties or wins and is refused, a member built only of type selectors loses and is ignored — and that split is load-bearing in **both** directions: refuse everything that could match and the live `p { color: … }` reds the suite; ignore the ancestor compounds and `.entry-feedback p` does. But it decides only the **normal** declarations, and a refusal resting on it alone is the same fail-open one mechanism over: `!important` beats every normal declaration whatever the selector, so a member that *loses* the comparison still renders — and losing is exactly what keeps it out of `blocks`, so `remValue`'s own `!important` refusal never sees it. `p { margin-top: 0 !important }`, one word added to the rule this sheet already carries, went green against the real stylesheet with the buttons flush against the panel. A claimed member is therefore refused on **either** ground, tested on the harvested body (`ruleBlock` is not told which property is coming, so an unrelated `p { color: … !important }` reds too — the usual trade). Ancestors are matched loosely — any ancestor, any order — because the tight version is a selector engine, and *that* looseness only over-refuses. It is loose about **which** ancestor, never about whether the compound describes an ancestor at all, and the version that read "any **combinator**" was wrong in the fail-*open* direction: the split discarded which combinator it split on, so a `+`/`~` compound — which names a **sibling**, never present in the ancestor set — came out unreachable and was neither collected nor refused. `.entry-hsi + p { margin-top: 0 }` is (0,1,1) against the flat class's (0,1,0), and the label **is** the `<p>` immediately after `.entry-hsi` — the very adjacency `entry-drill.test.ts` pins — so it went green against the real stylesheet at 18/18 with the buttons flush against the panel. Sibling combinators are refused now, gated on the subject reaching the element so an unrelated `.a + .b` still reads. Fixing it required fixing the **split**, not just adding a check: splitting on the bare character also shredded `:nth-child(2n+1)` and `[class~="x"]`, whose `+`/`~` combine nothing, leaving the subject as a fragment (`1)`) that names nothing and so reached nothing — the same silent pass by another route, unreachable from the sibling refusal. The split masks bracketed and parenthesised runs first (one-character filler, indexed in lockstep with the original, and the compounds are slices of the original so `denies` still sees `[hidden]` whole). Writing that lesson up as a fact about `+` and `~` is how the **identical** bug survived one function up, in the prelude split, for six revisions: a comma is the third separator with an in-parentheses spelling that separates nothing, because `:is()`/`:where()`/`:not()` all take an argument list. `:is(.entry-drill, .nope) p` (0,1,1) came apart into `:is(.entry-drill` and `.nope) p`, neither of which names the label, so it was neither collected nor refused; it and `.entry-drill p:not(.a, .b)` (0,2,1) each went green against the real stylesheet with the buttons flush against the panel, while `:where(p, div)` happened to refuse by luck of which fragment landed where. Both splits now share one `maskRuns`, and the general statement — *every separator in this grammar also has a spelling inside `[…]` or `(…)` where it separates nothing* — lives there rather than in either caller. `:is()` grouping is the natural way to give two screens' `<p>` one margin rule, i.e. a likely edit. That `context` answers **names**, though, and a compound can also select on a **condition** — `:nth-of-type(4)`, `:not(.x)`, `[id]` — which no set of names decides; the version that shipped the fix read an unlisted token as "cannot match" and said so in a comment resting on the DOM-completeness claim, which covers tags, ids and classes and stops there. `.entry-drill p:not(.x)` and `.entry-drill p:nth-of-type(4)` each outrank the flat class rule and each zero the label's margin, and both went green against the real stylesheet. So an undecidable token now **refuses** instead of exempting, and `Context.denies` is the escape hatch for the two live bare-condition subjects in this sheet (`:root`, `[hidden]`) — a *DOM assertion*, pinned in the same `entry-drill.test.ts` test as the names, not a convenience list, and it answers the **subject** only, since `:root p` genuinely reaches the label through an ancestor. Cite the pin, never the list's contents: staleness is loud (the next bare pseudo-class rule throws until someone answers it), and that loudness is the whole design. A **nested style rule** is the other fail-*open* one: the body group `[^}]*` cannot cross a `}` but crosses a `{` freely, so a match containing a nested rule stops at that rule's inner `}` and swallows it — never matched, silently dropped, leaving the ratio green on a length that no longer renders, while a *later* nested rule **is** matched and read as unconditional. Which line of the block someone typed on decided which way the guard was wrong. It is therefore refused like a specificity conflict, not parsed. Refuse the **shape** — a harvested body still containing `{` — never a list of at-rule names: the swallow needs no at-rule at all, and the name-list version shipped with native nesting (`.entry-drill { & .entry-answers-label { … } }`) and `@scope` walking straight through it, both reproduced green against the real stylesheet with the buttons flush against the panel. Nesting is the *likeliest* such edit, being the modern default idiom. The shape refusal over-refuses (a `@keyframes` anywhere in the sheet now throws though it cannot touch this rule) and that is the trade: a false red costs a minute, a false green is the defect. Note the recurring failure — each version's docblock over-claimed its own coverage (at-rules "err toward failing rather than passing"; then the at-rule being "the only fail-open one"; then "every way of leaving it is loud" while any rule reaching the element by tag, id or `*` left silently; then the name-set read being "sound only because `context` is asserted complete against the DOM", true of names and false of `:nth-of-type(4)`), and that claim is the dangerous half, because it tells the next session the one edit that reaches the defect is already covered. Note the *shape* of the miss as well: every time, the docblock either enumerated the spellings it knew and called the list closed, or named the one mechanism it had modelled and reasoned as though it were the only one (the fifth was "a member built only from type selectors loses and is ignored" — true of normal declarations, false of `!important`; the sixth was "any ancestor, any **combinator**, any order", true of the descendant and child combinators it had modelled and false of the two it had not). **State the mechanism, not the enumeration; and ask whether it is the *only* mechanism, not just whether it is modelled correctly.** The same helper's `remValue` had the twin hole from the other side: `!important` is the one declaration source order does not decide, so an earlier `margin-top: 0 !important` rendered while the last-declaration read reported the 1.5rem below it — refused now, like the shorthand case. One `IMPORTANT` regex serves both refusals, for the `COLOUR_LITERAL` reason: two hand-written copies drift and the half nobody edits waves the override through. There are **three** ways one property sets another and `OVERRIDES` modelled two: a shorthand (`margin`, and `all`), a logical equivalent (`margin-block-start`), and a **legacy alias** — the missing one. Browsers still honour `grid-gap`/`grid-row-gap`/`grid-column-gap` and alias them onto the modern names on flex containers too, which `.entry-answers` is, so an unrecognised name was filtered out and the read reported the `gap` above it: `.entry-answers { grid-column-gap: 2rem }` left the ratio comparing 1.5 to 0.5 and passing, with the buttons 2rem apart under a 1.5rem separation — the invariant inverted, suite green. That set is closed *per property* only because CSS gives these no other name; it is not a list to append to when a test goes red. Mutation-confirmed 2026-08-11: each mechanism alone reds the named test, and every escape appended to the real stylesheet reds the ratio.
- Every screen's no-argument render path — real localStorage, real clock, `Date.now()` seed — is exercised only by `main.test.ts`'s default-path test. Every other UI test injects `{ store, seed }`, so without it a screen that throws on the path the browser actually takes leaves `#app` showing only the router's home-link nav over an empty container, with the suite green.

## Conventions
- Courses/headings live in [0, 360) inside the engine (360 rejected); display formatting (e.g., 0 shown as 360°) is the UI's job.
- Screens register in `src/main.ts` (the route registry lives there, not `router.ts`); routes are `#/<path>` (the home screen links use the leading slash; the router accepts both forms).
- The router owns all chrome around screens: `renderCurrent` renders a `<nav>` home link (`a.home-link`, `href="#/"`, label "← Home" — `router.test.ts` pins the DOM label and README's mention against one `HOME_LABEL` constant) on every non-home screen and passes each screen a fresh child `<div>`, never `#app` itself, so a screen's `el.innerHTML = …` cannot clobber the nav. Screens must not add their own home/back links or reach outside the element they're given — guarded by the source scan in `router.test.ts`, which sweeps every shipping `.ts` under `src/` (not just `src/modules/` — the registry pulls `anki-export` from `src/app/`) and rejects `home-link` and a home `href` in any quoting, with `router.ts` the sole allowlisted carrier.
- localStorage schema is versioned (`{ v: 1, ... }` in `src/app/store.ts`); keep it backward compatible.
- Display formatting of any bearing goes through `courseText` in `src/format.ts` — three digits, 0 shown as 360. Interpolating `${deg}°` directly is how "the 20° radial … inbound course 200°" ends up in one sentence. A **distance to the fix** goes through `distanceText` in the same file — always one decimal plus `NM`, throwing on negatives and non-finites, because `(-0.04).toFixed(1)` is `"-0.0"` and a readout saying minus zero miles looks like a value. It is shared by the HSI's nav status line, that panel's `aria-label` and the entry drill's scenario prose so one aircraft cannot be described by two roundings on one screen (the generator quantises `distanceNm` to the same 0.1 NM for the same reason). It is deliberately **not** a universal distance formatter: leg lengths (`chartNote`) and the wind lab's cross-track carry their own resolutions on purpose, so don't "fix" those to it.
- Practice fixture names (`PUBLISHED_HOLDS`, `VOR_NAMES`, `NDB_NAMES` in `src/engine/clearance.ts`; `FIXES` in `src/modules/wind-lab.ts`) invent course, turn direction and leg length. A new name must be disjoint from `CHART_HOLDS` in `src/content/questions.ts`, from real waypoints (confirm no record at `opennav.com/waypoint/US/<NAME>` — a real fix renders a `<NAME> waypoint | OpenNav` title page, a free name renders the bare shell), **and from every other fixture list**. That last one is not pedantry: the first rename moved `FIXES` off real waypoints and straight onto `ZORVU`/`QUIBO`/`VAXNU`, which are `PUBLISHED_HOLDS` fixtures, so one name carried 300°/right on the entry-drill plate and a different invented hold in the wind lab. Any rendered plate line stays labelled fictitious (`chartNote` in `src/modules/entry-drill.ts`; the scenario line in `src/modules/wind-lab.ts`). Guarded by tests in `clearance.test.ts`, `entry-drill.test.ts` and `wind-lab.test.ts` — every fixture list needs its own guard, because the two that had one did not stop `FIXES` from shipping the real waypoints WINDY, DRIFT and TIMBR.
- `README.md` is an asserted artifact, not just prose: `dashboard.test.ts` imports it with `?raw` and diffs its module → exam-phase table against `PHASE_PREP` in `src/modules/dashboard.ts`. Keep the row shape ``| `#/<route>` | <Module> | ✓ | | |`` (the `#/dashboard` row is the only exempt one); renaming a route, reflowing that table, or adding a screen to `PHASE_PREP` without editing README in the same commit fails `npm test`. Same runtime-only doc↔code coupling class as the drill ids below.
- Drill ids (`entry`, `entry-learn`, `written`, `written-exam`, `oral`, `wind-lab`, `clearance-copy`, `to-from`) are the store's keys and are matched by string in two places: `ACTIVITY` in `src/modules/dashboard.ts` and `missesToCards` in `src/app/anki-export.ts`. A new drill that isn't added to both is silently invisible on the dashboard and exports no cards. `dashboard.test.ts` drives every controller and asserts each key it writes appears on the dashboard — the coupling is runtime-only, so a source grep can't see it (`written-test.ts` records through `DRILL_IDS[mode]`).
- A miss snapshot is a **cache, not a record**, and the read side must re-resolve it. `oral-prep.ts`/`written-test.ts`/`entry-drill.ts` denormalize the item's prose (and the entry drill's `determineEntry` output) into localStorage so a card survives its item being deleted — but that also *freezes the fact*, and nothing revisited it, so a miss recorded before a correction re-exported the pre-correction answer forever. That is the negative-training path the app exists to close, surviving *inside* the app. `missesToCards` (`src/app/anki-export.ts`) therefore rebuilds: bank cards from the live `ORAL_ITEMS`/`QUESTIONS` item by the `itemId`/`questionId` the snapshot records, and the entry answer from `determineEntry(heading, holdSpec)`, falling back to the snapshot only when the id or hold no longer resolves. Consequences: (a) `OralItem.id` and `WrittenQuestion.id` are now **store keys** as well as content keys, matched by string across the localStorage boundary exactly like the drill ids above — renaming one silently retires every recorded miss of that item back to its frozen prose, and no test compares the two sides, so alias or migrate; (b) the entry *scenario text* stays frozen on purpose — it is re-derivable only from `seed`, which maps through the fixture lists, so after a fixture rename the same seed yields a different hold and the card would swap the question the user actually missed. Every rebuilt field needs a test whose stale fixture **differs from live in that field**: the first version copied the live values into all but one field and five of six rebuilds reverted with the suite green.
- `renderHSI` (`src/ui/hsi.ts`) has **two** consumers with different prop contracts, and the second one is easy to forget: the entry drill passes `heading`, `course`, `headingBug` and `nav: { fix, distanceNm }` on every render, plus `thumbOverlay` in learn mode and `sectorOverlay` in learn mode or once an answer is graded; the written test's entry figure (`src/modules/written-test.ts`) passes `heading` and `course` only. Count the overlays as production props — they are the whole learn-mode teaching aid and the reason `OVERLAY_ALPHA`'s ceiling/floor tuning and the `OVER_WEDGE` map exist, so a reader who takes the four-prop list as exhaustive concludes they are test-only and retunes them freely. That second case is the **no-nav contract** and it is specified, not incidental — the course pointer is still drawn, and the deviation bar, its dots, the TO/FROM flag and the status line are all suppressed. Two *separate* contracts, easily conflated: absent `nav` suppresses that list and nothing else; absent `headingBug` is what suppresses the bug and the HDG box (`renderHSI({ heading, course, headingBug })` with no `nav` draws both). The written test omits both props, which is why one sentence used to claim all six. The bar is not *centred*: a centred bar reads as "established on course" on a graded question, which is why the pointer leaves its middle empty rather than drawing a full-length line — and that **gap** is the load-bearing half, since drawing the stem as one continuous line through the rose centre renders exactly the picture the contract forbids while every "the bar is absent" assertion still passes (`leaves the pointer’s centre empty, wide enough for the bar that slides there` — typographic apostrophe, as the title is written — measures the gap against a rendered bar, not a retyped constant). The `aria-label` is the panel in one string (`role="img"` hides the internals), so it carries the deviation reading, the `GPS OBS` annunciation and — read **last**, after the instrument — the two learn-mode overlays, as well as the digits: the sector arcs through `courseText` because they ride the card and are magnetic, the thumb band as bare degrees off the nose (deliberately *not* `courseText`, which would pad `70` to `070°` and read as a heading). A drawn mark is the field a label loses silently, and the overlays are the half of learn mode no readout repeats. `nav` without `course` throws. Both consumers share the `.hsi` rule in `styles.css` and the panel viewBox, so any `LAYOUT` or aspect-ratio change resizes the written-test figure too — check it deliberately rather than discovering it. `written-test.test.ts` is a regression surface for every HSI change, not just CSS ones. Two naming traps in that file, both of which have cost a wrong assertion: `hsi-heading-*` is the **current** heading (what the aircraft is doing) while `hsi-hdg-*`/`hsi-crs-*` are the **selected**-value boxes; and those six class names are built from an interpolated `class="hsi-${kind}-…"`, so grepping the source for a `class="hsi-…"` literal reports them absent. Read the rendered DOM, not the file, before concluding a selector does not exist.
- The entry drill's scenario carries a **position**, not just a heading. `distanceNm` comes from a second `rng()` call **appended after** the heading draw in `generateScenario` (`src/modules/entry-drill.ts`) so every existing seed keeps its heading — stored misses and Anki cards are keyed by seed, and shifting the stream rewrites history (pinned by `keeps every existing seed on its original heading and answer`). The window is `CLEARANCE_LEAD_MINUTES`/`SPEED_REDUCTION_MINUTES` × `NOMINAL_GROUNDSPEED_KT`, the first two cited verbatim (`FAA JO 7110.65BB 4-6-1 c.4` — it is `c.4`, **not** `g`; and `AIM 5-3-8 d`), the groundspeed a **training assumption that must never become a citation**. Distance is quantised to the 0.1 NM `distanceText` renders, so the printed number and the needle come from one value. It is display state only: `determineEntry` takes no distance, so "distance does not reach the answer key" is true by signature and an assertion of it could not fail. Consequence recorded as **accepted** in `docs/content-audit.md`: at 6–10 NM with `FULL_SCALE_NM = 1` the needle is pegged unless the heading is within ~6–10° of the course or its reciprocal. That is the physics of being cleared to hold while not established — do not shorten the distances to make the needle livelier.
- Colours live in exactly two places: the `:root` custom properties in `src/styles.css` (the light UI palette) and the frozen `PALETTE` in `src/ui/palette.ts` (the SVG instrument palette, imported by `hsi.ts` and `mapview.ts`). A colour literal anywhere else fails `npm test`, in any notation — `src/contrast.ts` exports one `COLOUR_LITERAL` pattern (hex plus `rgb()`/`hsl()`/`oklch()`/`color-mix()`…) used by both suites, because two hand-written copies of "what a colour looks like" drift and the half nobody edits waves colours through. `styles.test.ts` asserts every colour in the stylesheet sits inside `:root`; `palette.test.ts` sweeps **every** `src/**/*.ts` via `import.meta.glob(…'?raw')` against a three-entry allowlist (`contrast.ts`, `contrast.test.ts`, `palette.ts`), so a screen under `modules/` cannot hand-colour a verdict line either; it also scans `index.html`, the one artifact neither sweep reaches, for literals and for any inline `style=` that could carry a named colour. Named colours (`crimson`, `fill="red"`) are the notation `COLOUR_LITERAL` cannot see, because matching bare words against TS prose and identifiers is all false positives — so each suite closes them where its own grammar is bounded, and nowhere by guessing: the stylesheet scans **declaration values** (comments and quoted strings stripped) against `CSS_NAMED_COLOURS` in `src/contrast.ts`, the spec-closed CSS Color 4 §6.1 list; the two instrument modules constrain every `fill=`/`stroke=` attribute to `none` or an interpolation, and must import `./palette` (the no-literal half alone passes for a file that renders no colour). `color: crimson` passed every guard in `styles.test.ts` until the first of those landed. Relatedly, `:root` values are harvested generically and each must be a `#hex` or a length — a `--warn: orange` the hex-only harvest could not see was a colour nothing classified and nothing measured. Both harvests treat the trailing `;` as optional (`(?=[;}])`), because CSS lets the last declaration of a block drop it and requiring one read straight past `{ …; color: crimson }` — the exact notation the named-colour scan exists to catch. `HEX_COLOUR_SOURCE` is exported from `contrast.ts` for the same no-second-copy reason as `COLOUR_LITERAL`: a local `{3,8}` copy in both suites accepted `#rrggbbaa`, so the test named "a hex the oracle can read" passed and the throw surfaced from inside the contrast test instead.
- `PALETTE` is **two frozen groups**, not one flat map: `plate.*` (the light, chart-like plan view) and `pfd.*` (the dark G1000 panel). One `face` colour could not serve two instruments that want opposite backdrops, which is what forced the split. The three entry colours therefore exist **twice**, and `keeps each entry colour the same hue on both instruments` (`hueOf` in `src/contrast.ts`, 5° tolerance) is what stops "green means direct" becoming two colour codes on one screen — the HSI and the map render together in the feedback view, which is the drift `PALETTE` was centralised to prevent. `is frozen all the way down` walks the tree rather than hand-listing groups: `Object.freeze` is shallow, so a hand-listed check leaves the next group added unfrozen by default. Same exhaustiveness lesson as `TIERS`, and it applies to `leaves()` too — it must recurse, or every 3-deep `pfd.entry.*` colour ships unmeasured.
- Every colour must clear WCAG 4.5:1 where it renders text and 3:1 where it renders a line or shape (SC 1.4.3 / 1.4.11, unrounded), computed with `contrastRatio` in `src/contrast.ts` rather than trusting the eye — against every backdrop its group actually renders on (`--bg` and `--surface` for UI colours; for instrument colours the backdrop is chosen **per palette group**: `plate.*` against `plate.face` **and** `--bg`, since `.mapview` carries no background rule and the plate rect's rounded corners and 1 px inset leave its marks sitting straight on the page, but `pfd.*` against `pfd.face` alone, because the panel rect fills the whole viewBox so no PFD mark reaches the page — plus `pfd.face` itself against `--bg` at 3:1, SC 1.4.11, the dark plate being its own component boundary and needing no bezel). That group→backdrop map needs its own exhaustiveness test for exactly the reason `TIERS` does: a hand-written table measures only the groups that existed when it was typed. Each suite has a `TIERS` map classifying every role `text`/`graphic`/`backdrop` and a test that fails on any palette entry missing from it: a hand-listed set of roles measures only what existed when the list was typed, so the next colour added ships unmeasured. Hence `inkDim` is a line-only colour — the map's north arrow letter had to move to `ink` because 3.61:1 is legal for the arrow and illegal for the `N`.
- A translucent mark is not the colour it is drawn with, so alphas live in `OVERLAY_ALPHA` (`src/ui/palette.ts`) and `palette.test.ts` composites with `blend` before measuring. An alpha inlined in `hsi.ts` is one the suite silently certifies at full strength: a sector fill at `0.16` with a `stroke-opacity` of `0.45` renders at 1.27:1 and 2.03:1 on the white face — and drops the minor ticks crossing a wedge to 2.85:1 — while the test reported the opaque 6.14:1. The wedge boundary is therefore stroked opaque and only the fill is a tint. Two guards keep that true rather than one: every `*opacity=` attribute in the instrument modules must interpolate `${OVERLAY_ALPHA.…}` (banning `stroke-opacity` alone left `fill-opacity="0.5"` free to ship while the suite kept measuring `0.1`), and every key of `OVERLAY_ALPHA` must appear in the composited-assertion list — a third alpha would otherwise satisfy the first guard with nothing measuring it, the same exhaustiveness hole `TIERS` closes for roles. Same rule for CSS: `--surface` on `--bg` is 1.13:1, so `button`/`.module-link` carry an explicit `1px solid var(--border)` edge rather than relying on the fill difference (SC 1.4.11 wants 3:1 of a component's boundary). `OVERLAY_ALPHA` stays **flat** through the palette split on purpose: `mapview.ts` uses no alphas at all, so a `plate` group would be empty, and nesting makes `Object.keys(OVERLAY_ALPHA)` return *group names* — blinding the very exhaustiveness guard above. (Its `tints` must also be computed **inside** the test body, not at describe scope: `blend` throws on a non-numeric alpha, so the nesting mutation used to throw during collection and the file reported "no tests" with the named guard never running. A red suite that names nothing is not a guard.)
- An alpha is tuned to a **face**, not to a palette, and inverting the backdrop inverts which way a tint moves it. The `sectorFill` that was a *ceiling* on white paper (0.1) rendered the wedge at 1.15:1 on the near-black panel — a teaching aid that is not there — while every existing assertion passed, because all of them were ceilings. So a *tint* needs a **floor** as well (`tints the wedge enough to be seen at all`, 1.3:1). That floor is deliberately **not** an SC threshold and must not become one: a fill reaching 3:1 against the face on its own needs α ≈ 0.44, which puts the dim ticks crossing it at 2.2:1. The fill and the boundary cannot both carry the sector — that is why the boundary is stroked opaque and only the fill is a tint, and it is arithmetic, not style. Raising a tint lowers the contrast of *everything* drawn over it, so the over-a-wedge classification is its own exhaustive map (`OVER_WEDGE` in `palette.test.ts`) and cannot reuse `TIERS`: `pfd.course` is text in the CRS box on bare face but only a line on the card, and measuring it at its `TIERS` tier would demand 4.5:1 of a needle. `thumbBand` is the opposite case and generalising the floor to it inverts the advice: it is exempt from `OVER_WEDGE` only because at 0.9 it **occludes** the marks in its arc, and its own graphic-tier assertion gains contrast as the alpha *rises*, so it passes all the way down to 0.6 while the ticks under it return as ghosts nothing measures. What holds it up is a **ceiling on what still shows through** (`hides the card marks it covers rather than half-tinting them`, `OCCLUSION_CEILING = 1.5`, chosen and not an SC threshold) — over everything the band's 75–84 annulus is drawn across, which is both tick weights and the card ring **and the sector wedges' opaque boundary strokes**, the group a hand-written `['card','cardDim','chrome']` omitted under a comment calling itself exhaustive; the entry colours are spread from `PALETTE.pfd.entry` so a fourth is measured by default, and what no assertion there can carry is that the list is a claim about `hsi.ts`'s `LAYOUT` radii — plus `is thick enough to cover the card marks palette.test.ts says it hides` in `hsi.test.ts` — the radial-span half, since a band thinned to a hairline crosses no tick and that whole premise measures a picture the panel does not draw. But an `OVER_WEDGE` exemption is about the marks *under* a mark and says nothing about that mark's own backdrop, and conflating the two hid the tightest composite on the panel: learn mode draws **both** overlays, the band is emitted after `.hsi-rose` closes and its annulus overlaps the wedges across 75–80, so it renders on a **tint**, at 3.38:1 — below the 3.79:1 dim ticks that `sectorFill`'s docblock called binding, and 3:1 falls at α ≈ 0.29, not the 0.32 documented. Tuning to that documented ceiling would have shipped 2.79:1 green, because the band's only contrast assertion composited it against the *bare* face. So a mark that is exempt from `OVER_WEDGE` still needs measuring **over a wedge** whenever it is drawn over one (`keeps the thumb band above the graphic tier over a tinted sector`, which also asserts it is the tighter of the two readings so it cannot decay into a copy of the bare-face one), and the paint order it rests on is a **cross-group** fact no radius check sees — `is painted over the sector wedges, not under them`. Finally, `palette.test.ts` composites the colours it *thinks* are drawn and reads **both instrument modules** only as source text — its scans are satisfied by a wedge painted in `pfd.card` at the band alpha, and then the measured picture and the drawn one are different pictures. Each instrument needs its own drawn-vs-measured tie: `hsi.test.ts`'s `overlay paint` block for `pfd`, `mapview.test.ts`'s `paint (…)` block for `plate` — the plate half shipped open, and a fix label or north `N` repainted `plate.inkDim` renders two letters at 3.61:1 while the suite reports `inkDim` compliant at its 3:1 graphic tier. Those blocks are also what makes a `TIERS` entry a claim about the drawing rather than about the comment beside it (`plate.readout` was demoted `text` → `graphic` on the strength of one).
- The literal hexes in `contrast.ts`, `contrast.test.ts` and `palette.test.ts` are the deliberate exception (the allowlist above): they are hand-checkable reference pairs (`#767676` on white = 4.542 pins the gamma exponent), and importing expected values from the palette would make the oracle circular. Vite excludes the importing module from its own `import.meta.glob`, which is why `palette.test.ts` needs no allowlist entry for itself.
